Let's clarify the terms you've mentioned and the one you might be forgetting:

Association: This is a general term representing a relationship between two separate classes which establish through their objects. It describes how objects of different classes are related and interact with each other. It's a very broad term and can encompass other more specific relationships. Association doesn't imply any strong ownership or lifecycle dependency.

Composition: This is a strong form of association that represents a "has-a" relationship where one object (the "whole") owns another object (the "part"), and the part cannot exist independently of the whole. If the whole object is destroyed, the part object is also destroyed. This implies a strong lifecycle dependency.

Aggregation: This is a weaker form of association that also represents a "has-a" relationship, but in this case, the "part" object can exist independently of the "whole" object. It implies a weaker lifecycle dependency. If the whole object is destroyed, the part object can still exist.

To summarize the relationships:

Inheritance ("is-a"): Represents a type hierarchy where a subclass inherits from a superclass.
Association ("has-a" - general): A general relationship between objects.
Composition ("has-a" - strong): A strong "part-of" relationship with a strong lifecycle dependency (e.g., a car has an engine; if the car is destroyed, the specific engine instance is also typically considered gone with it).
Aggregation ("has-a" - weak): A weaker "part-of" relationship where the part can exist independently (e.g., a department has students; if the department closes, the students still exist).
Dependency ("uses-a" or "creates-a"): One object uses another object. The used object can be passed as an argument, created within a method, or be a local variable. The lifecycle dependency is usually weaker than in aggregation or composition.
While "creates-a" describes an interaction, the primary ways classes relate in terms of structure and object relationships are often categorized as inheritance, association (with its specializations composition and aggregation), and dependency.
